r"""
The Gauss-Newton method: the mode of the posterior of a problem with a forward model.

With a forward model :math:`F` (:class:`~fips.ForwardModel`) the observations
are :math:`z = F(x) + c + e`, and the posterior is not Gaussian. Its mode, the
estimate, is the state where the cost

.. math::

    \chi^2(x) = (x - x_0)^T S_0^{-1} (x - x_0)
        + \gamma\, (z - F(x) - c)^T S_z^{-1} (z - F(x) - c)

is least. ``_gauss_newton`` finds it as Rodgers (2000, chapter 5) does:
from the prior (and, with ``starts``, from draws of it too: ``_starts``), each step solves the problem linearized at the last estimate
(:meth:`Problem.linearized <fips.Problem.linearized>`, with the Jacobian
:math:`J_i` at :math:`x_i`) by the analytic method,

.. math::

    x_{i+1} = x_0 + K_i \big(z - F(x_i) - c + J_i (x_i - x_0)\big),

and it stops when the step is small in the metric of the posterior
covariance of that solve (Rodgers' rule, :math:`d_i^2 \ll n`):

.. math::

    d_i^2 = (x_{i+1} - x_i)^T \hat{S}_i^{-1} (x_{i+1} - x_i)
        < \mathrm{tol} \cdot n, \qquad
    \hat{S}_i^{-1} = S_0^{-1} + \gamma J_i^T S_z^{-1} J_i.

A step that overshoots, so that the cost at its estimate is higher than at
the last one, is halved until it is not (a line search on the cost; the
mode is the same), and only a whole step below the rule stops the
iterations. The rule measures the step, not the distance to the mode: when
the steps fall by a constant factor near 1 (a model that bends much over the
posterior's spread), the solve logs a warning that the last iterate may be
further from the mode (``_warn_if_slow``).

For a transform (:class:`~fips.LogNormalForward`,
:class:`~fips.SoftplusForward`), whose second derivatives are diagonal, the
cost's whole Hessian is :math:`\hat{S}_i^{-1} - D_i`, with :math:`D_i =
\gamma \operatorname{diag}(T''(u) \circ H^T S_z^{-1} r)` on the block
(``ForwardModel._curvature``), and each iteration also takes Newton's step,
solved by conjugate gradients preconditioned by :math:`\hat{S}_i`
(``_newton``): it goes to whichever of the two estimates has the lower cost
(each halved as above), and Gauss-Newton's alone where the Newton matrix is
not positive definite. The iterations stop when both steps are below the
rule, with Gauss-Newton's, so the mode is the same and the Gaussian at the
mode is still the analytic solution of the problem linearized at the last
iterate. Newton converges quadratically where Gauss-Newton crawls: where the
model bends much over the posterior's spread, it takes a fraction of
Gauss-Newton's steps. A model fips did not write has no curvature, and its
steps are Gauss-Newton's.

:math:`\hat{S}_i^{-1}` is never formed. Every estimate is :math:`x_0 + S_0 y`
with :math:`y = J_i^T u_i` (:math:`u_i` the analytic solve's :math:`(J_i S_0
J_i^T + S_z/\gamma)^{-1}` times its innovation), so the prior term of
:math:`d^2` is :math:`\Delta y^T S_0 \Delta y` (no inverse of :math:`S_0`,
which may be singular) and its data term :math:`\gamma (J_i \delta)^T
S_z^{-1} (J_i \delta)`, a solve with the observation covariance factored once.

The solution (:class:`GaussNewton`) keeps the analytic solution of the last
linearized problem, the Gaussian at the mode: the analytic posterior of the
problem linearized at the last iterate, one step of :math:`d^2 <
\mathrm{tol} \cdot n` from the mode. Its estimate is the mode, and its gain,
DOFS and information content are those of the Gaussian at the mode. Its
variances, the covariance of aggregates, the mean, the intervals and its draws
are those of the posterior itself, :math:`p(x \mid z) \propto e^{-\chi^2(x)/2}`, drawn by
Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (``_Sampler``): a Gaussian about the mode is its mass
and its exactly solved part, the rest of the cost its kicks, and a Metropolis
test keeps the chain exact. That Gaussian is the Gaussian at the mode where
the posterior is that Gaussian (a linear model), and otherwise the one of the
same linearized problem solved at a thirtieth of :math:`\gamma`, wider where
the data pin the state down, so that the paths reach the far ends of a
posterior that bends along a curve. Several chains run from spread starts.
Where they disagree on an element (a split R-hat), the sampler widens its
Gaussian in the few directions their draws spread past it and draws again,
and the solve warns if they still disagree: one chain that never enters
a tail of a curved posterior reports too small a spread, and cannot tell.
The covariance of aggregates warns when they disagree on an aggregate that
is read, which depends on how the elements vary together.
Where the model bends over the posterior's
spread, the Gaussian at the mode is not the posterior (a sum of lognormal
values from its draws runs high and covers the truth too rarely); the draws
are. The misfits and the chi-squared are the cost's terms at the mode, from
the model itself. With a forward operator (a linear problem)
``_gauss_newton`` returns the analytic solution itself.

References
----------
Rodgers, C. D. (2000). *Inverse Methods for Atmospheric Sounding: Theory and
Practice*, chapter 5. World Scientific. https://doi.org/10.1142/3171

Shahbaba, B., Lan, S., Johnson, W. O., and Neal, R. M. (2014). Split
Hamiltonian Monte Carlo. *Statistics and Computing*, 24(3), 339-349.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s11222-012-9373-1

Hoffman, M. D., and Gelman, A. (2014). The No-U-Turn sampler: adaptively
setting path lengths in Hamiltonian Monte Carlo. *Journal of Machine Learning
Research*, 15, 1593-1623.

Vehtari, A., Gelman, A., Simpson, D., Carpenter, B., and Bürkner, P.-C.
(2021). Rank-normalization, folding, and localization: an improved R-hat for
assessing convergence of MCMC. *Bayesian Analysis*, 16(2), 667-718.
https://doi.org/10.1214/20-BA1221

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class GaussNewtonOptions:
    r"""
    The options of the ``"gauss_newton"`` method.

    Parameters
    ----------
    gamma : float, default 1.0
        The regularization factor :math:`\gamma`, as for the analytic method
        (:class:`~fips.methods.analytic.AnalyticOptions`): the observation
        covariance is divided by it.
    max_iterations : int, default 50
        The most steps to take. A solve that has not stopped by then raises.
        A mildly nonlinear problem stops in a few; a strongly nonlinear one
        can take tens, its steps' ``d2`` falling by a constant factor.
    tol : float, default 1e-6
        The step at which the iterations stop: when :math:`d^2`, the step
        measured in the posterior covariance, is below ``tol`` times the
        number of state elements :math:`n`. Rodgers' rule asks for
        :math:`d^2 \ll n`; at the default the last step is a thousandth of a
        posterior standard deviation (root mean square), and the estimate is
        within about that of the mode: far inside its uncertainty, for an
        iteration or two more than a looser rule. Each step costs a factoring
        of the problem linearized at the last estimate.
    draws : int, default 2000
        The number of draws of the posterior kept for its variances and the
        covariance of aggregates, from all the chains together (Monte Carlo
        estimates: the relative error of a standard deviation is about
        :math:`\sqrt{\tau / (2\, \text{draws}})`, with :math:`\tau` the
        draws' autocorrelation time). An element the observations see only
        through a sum with others, on a posterior that bends, mixes slowly:
        the chains then disagree on its spread, and the solve warns; raise
        `draws`. Chains that agree are not proof: each draw moves about one
        width of the Gaussian at the mode, and every chain can miss an arm
        of a curved posterior that lies several widths along it. The draws
        are made at
        the first use of the variances or covariances, and each trial of
        :meth:`Posterior.trials <fips.Posterior.trials>` makes its own, so
        fewer make trials quicker. There is no setting without draws: the
        Gaussian at the mode, which is not the posterior where the model
        bends, is ``fips.solve(problem.linearized(post.estimate))``.
    burn_in : int, default 200
        The draws each chain makes and leaves out before those it keeps, from
        its start, while it tunes its step to an acceptance rate of 0.9.
        Where the model bends and the chains' kept draws disagree on an
        element (a split R-hat above 1.05), the sampler's Gaussian is
        widened where they spread past it, each chain tunes its step again
        over half a burn-in from its last draw, and the kept draws are made
        again: such a solve costs about twice the model runs or more.
    seed : int, default 0
        The seed of the starts, the tuning and the kept draws, so that a
        solve is reproducible. Bit for bit only with NumPy 2.0.2 or later:
        before it, on a CPU with AVX-512, NumPy's ``exp`` and ``log`` can
        differ in the last bit by where their output lies in memory, and
        the chains grow that into other draws.
    chains : int, default 4
        The number of chains the draws are split among. Each starts from a
        draw of the Gaussian at the mode and tunes its own step over its own
        burn-in. Chains that disagree, by a split R-hat
        above 1.05 on at least 5% of the elements (any one, at 20 or fewer;
        fewer are logged at INFO), are a warning that one chain cannot give: a chain that
        never enters a tail of a curved posterior reports a small spread and
        a small error together. Each chain costs a burn-in more; one chain is
        the sampler of fips 0.2.0b2, from the mode.
    starts : int, default 4
        The number of starts Gauss-Newton runs from: the prior mean and
        ``starts - 1`` draws of the prior. Where they end at modes more
        than one posterior standard deviation apart, the posterior has
        several, and the solve warns (with a loose `tol`, starts that end
        too near to tell are first solved again to a tighter one): its draws, split at the Gaussian at
        one mode, do not reach another, and chains that agree cannot show
        it. The estimate is then the mode of lowest cost; otherwise it is
        the prior mean's, the same as with one start. Each start costs a
        Gauss-Newton solve more, and a start that does not converge is
        logged and left out. On the reference suite's problems (12 to 900
        elements, 2026-11-21) four starts cost 3 to 8 times one start's
        solve, a few percent of the draws', and found other modes only where
        a softplus prior is wide (log standard deviation 1.5): an element
        either near zero, where the model is flat, or well above it. :meth:`Posterior.with_data
        <fips.Posterior.with_data>` (and so each trial of
        :meth:`Posterior.trials <fips.Posterior.trials>`) solves from the
        last linearization alone.

    Raises
    ------
    ValueError
        If `gamma` is not a positive, finite number, `max_iterations` is not a
        positive integer, `tol` is not a positive, finite number, `draws` is
        not an integer of at least 2, `burn_in` or `seed` is not a
        nonnegative one, `chains` or `starts` is not a positive one, or
        `draws` is fewer than two for each chain.
    """

    gamma: float = 1.0
    """The regularization factor: the observation covariance is divided by it."""
    max_iterations: int = 50
    """The most steps to take."""
    tol: float = 1e-6
    r"""The iterations stop when :math:`d^2 <` ``tol`` :math:`\cdot\, n`."""
    draws: int = 2000
    """The number of draws of the posterior kept for its variances and covariances."""
    burn_in: int = 200
    """The draws left out before those kept, while the sampler tunes its step."""
    seed: int = 0
    """The seed of the starts, the tuning and the kept draws."""
    chains: int = 4
    """The number of chains the draws are split among, each from its own start."""
    starts: int = 4
    """The number of starts of Gauss-Newton: the prior mean and draws of the prior."""

    def __post_init__(self):
        object.__setattr__(self, "gamma", AnalyticOptions(self.gamma).gamma)
        value = self.max_iterations
        if (
            not isinstance(value, numbers.Integral)
            or isinstance(value, bool)
            or value < 1
        ):
            raise ValueError(
                f"max_iterations must be a positive integer, got {value!r}."
            )
        object.__setattr__(self, "max_iterations", int(value))
        try:
            tol = float(self.tol)
        except (TypeError, ValueError):
            raise ValueError(f"tol must be a number, got {self.tol!r}.") from None
        if not (math.isfinite(tol) and tol > 0):
            raise ValueError(f"tol must be positive and finite, got {tol}.")
        object.__setattr__(self, "tol", tol)
        for name, least in (
            ("draws", 2),
            ("burn_in", 0),
            ("seed", 0),
            ("chains", 1),
            ("starts", 1),
        ):
            value = getattr(self, name)
            if (
                not isinstance(value, numbers.Integral)
                or isinstance(value, bool)
                or value < least
            ):
                kind = {2: "an", 1: "a positive", 0: "a nonnegative"}[least]
                at_least = (
                    " of at least 2 (a variance needs two draws)" if least == 2 else ""
                )
                raise ValueError(
                    f"{name} must be {kind} integer{at_least}, got {value!r}."
                )
            object.__setattr__(self, name, int(value))
        if self.draws < 2 * self.chains:
            raise ValueError(
                f"draws must be at least 2 for each of the {self.chains} chains, "
                f"got {self.draws}."
            )




@dataclass(frozen=True, eq=False)
class GaussNewton:
    r"""
    The Gauss-Newton method's solution: the mode, the Gaussian of the problem linearized at the last iterate, and draws of the posterior.

    It keeps the problem with the forward model (``problem``), the analytic
    solution of the last linearized problem (``analytic``, whose estimate is
    the mode), the state that problem was linearized at, and each step's
    :math:`d^2` and the cost after it. The :class:`~fips.Posterior` asks it
    for every array it reports: it is the method's
    :class:`~fips.posterior.Solution`. The draws of the posterior are made at
    their first use and kept (not pickled: a posterior read back draws them
    again, the same, from the seed). Its answers, by what each needs:

    - the estimate is the mode, not the mean, and is not linear in the
      observations (no ``estimate_is_mean``, no ``estimate_is_linear``);
    - the variances and the covariance of aggregates are those of the kept
      draws of the posterior (:meth:`kept`: ``draws`` of them, split among
      ``chains`` chains, each after a ``burn_in`` that tunes its step, from
      ``seed``), about their mean,
      and so are the posterior's mean and intervals (the draws' quantiles);
      :meth:`sample` draws more;
    - the gain, DOFS and the information content are those of the Gaussian
      at the mode (``analytic``'s); the posterior reads the averaging kernel
      and the innovation covariance from the linearized problem
      (``linearized``);
    - the misfits and the chi-squared are the terms of the cost at the mode,
      with the residual :math:`z - F(\hat{x}) - c` of the model itself;
    - :meth:`with_data` solves again, starting from the last linearization,
      and its draws are its own; there is no ``with_gamma``: the
      linearization depends on gamma.

    Every estimate is :math:`x_0 + S_0 y`, and the solution keeps the
    :math:`y` of the mode and of the last linearization point, so the state
    misfit, :math:`d^2` and the sampler need no inverse of :math:`S_0`.
    """

    evidence_is_laplace: ClassVar[bool] = True
    """Its log evidence is Laplace's: the cost at the mode and the information content of the Gaussian there (exact for a linear model)."""

    problem: Problem
    """The problem with the forward model."""
    analytic: "Whitened | Information"
    """The analytic solution of the problem linearized at :attr:`point`; its estimate is the mode."""
    mode: np.ndarray
    r"""The mode, :math:`\hat{x}`."""
    mode_y: np.ndarray
    r""":math:`y` with :math:`\hat{x} = x_0 + S_0 y`."""
    point: np.ndarray
    """The state of the last linearization, one step from the mode."""
    point_y: np.ndarray
    r""":math:`y` with :attr:`point` :math:`= x_0 + S_0 y`."""
    d2: np.ndarray
    r"""Each step's :math:`d^2`, in the metric of its solve's posterior covariance (a step taken at half its length has a quarter of its whole one's)."""
    cost: np.ndarray
    r"""The cost at each step's estimate, :math:`\gamma` times the data misfit plus the state misfit."""
    data_misfit: float
    r"""The data misfit at the mode, :math:`r^T S_z^{-1} r` with :math:`r = z - F(\hat{x}) - c`."""
    state_misfit: float
    r"""The state misfit at the mode, :math:`(\hat{x} - x_0)^T S_0^{-1} (\hat{x} - x_0) = y^T S_0 y`."""
    options: GaussNewtonOptions
    """The options it solved with (and :meth:`with_data` solves with)."""

    # -- the draws, kept outside the frozen fields -------------------------------

    def _sampler(self) -> "_Sampler":
        """Return the sampler of this solution's posterior, made once."""
        sampler = self.__dict__.get("_sampler_")
        if sampler is None:
            sampler = self.__dict__["_sampler_"] = _Sampler(self)
        return sampler

    def _burned_in(self) -> "tuple[_Tuning, ...]":
        """Return each chain's step, tuned over its burn-in from its start (each chain's generator a child of the seed's, apart from the kept draws'); with one chain, from the mode."""
        sampler, options = self._sampler(), self.options
        tunings = []
        for k, child in enumerate(
            np.random.SeedSequence(options.seed).spawn(options.chains)
        ):
            rng = np.random.default_rng(child)
            start = sampler.start(rng) if options.chains > 1 else None
            tuning = sampler.tune(options.burn_in, rng, start)
            _log_tuning(k, tuning, options.burn_in)
            tunings.append(tuning)
        return tuple(tunings)

    def _tunings(self) -> "tuple[_Tuning, ...]":
        """Return each chain's tuned step and where its tuning ended: those the kept draws were made with (:meth:`kept`, made first if they were not)."""
        self.kept()
        return self.__dict__["_tuning_"]

    def kept(self) -> np.ndarray:
        """Return the kept draws (``n x draws``, the chains side by side), which the variances, the covariances, the mean and the intervals are read from: drawn from the seed at the first call (twice where the chains disagree: :meth:`_redraw`), with a warning if they still disagree or mix too slowly for their variances."""
        kept = self.__dict__.get("_draws")
        if kept is None:
            rng = np.random.default_rng(self.options.seed)
            tunings = self._burned_in()
            kept, stats = self._run(tunings, self.options.draws, rng)
            if self._disagree(kept):
                tunings = self._redraw(kept, tunings)
                kept, stats = self._run(tunings, self.options.draws, rng)
            self.__dict__["_tuning_"] = tunings
            _warn_if_many_steps(tunings)
            kept = self.__dict__["_draws"] = _read_only(kept)
            self.__dict__["_stats"] = {
                name: _read_only(values) for name, values in stats.items()
            }
            lengths = self.lengths()
            _log_divergent(stats["diverging"], logger)
            _warn_if_unmixed(kept, lengths, logger, _UNMIXED_DRAWS)
            _warn_if_imprecise(kept, logger, _SLOW_DRAWS, lengths=lengths)
        return kept

    def _disagree(self, kept: np.ndarray) -> bool:
        """Return whether the chains of the draws `kept` disagree where the model bends, so that :meth:`kept` draws them again: a split R-hat above :data:`~fips._draws._UNMIXED` on any element, where the sampler is split at the wider Gaussian."""
        if not self._sampler().bends or self.options.chains < 2:
            return False
        rhat = _split_rhat(kept, self.lengths())
        count = int((rhat > _UNMIXED).sum())
        if count:
            logger.info(
                "The posterior's chains disagree on %d of %d state elements (split "
                "R-hat above %.2f, the worst %.3g): the sampler's Gaussian is "
                "refitted from their draws, and the draws made again.",
                count,
                len(rhat),
                _UNMIXED,
                float(rhat.max()),
            )
        return count > 0

    def _redraw(
        self, kept: np.ndarray, tunings: "tuple[_Tuning, ...]"
    ) -> "tuple[_Tuning, ...]":
        """
        Refit the sampler's Gaussian from the draws `kept` and return each chain's step tuned again, from its last draw, over half a burn-in.

        The refit reads at most :data:`_MOST_SEEN` of the draws, every k-th
        of each chain's (:meth:`_Sampler.refit`). Each chain's step starts
        from the one it was tuned to (`tunings`), and its generator is a
        child of the seed's after the probes'. The draws `kept` are left out,
        as the burn-in's are: the kept draws are made again from where they
        ended, with the refitted Gaussian fixed, so the chain stays exact.
        """
        sampler, options = self._sampler(), self.options
        every = -(-options.draws // _MOST_SEEN)
        ends = np.cumsum(self.lengths())
        chains = [
            kept[:, end - length : end]
            for length, end in zip(self.lengths(), ends, strict=True)
        ]
        sampler.refit(
            [[sampler.point(x) for x in chain[:, ::every].T] for chain in chains]
        )
        children = np.random.SeedSequence(options.seed).spawn(2 * options.chains + 1)
        again = []
        for k, (child, chain, tuning) in enumerate(
            zip(children[options.chains + 1 :], chains, tunings, strict=True)
        ):
            burn_in = max(options.burn_in // 2, 1)
            last = sampler.point(chain[:, -1])
            tuning = sampler.tune(
                burn_in, np.random.default_rng(child), last, tuning.step
            )
            _log_tuning(k, tuning, burn_in)
            again.append(tuning)
        return tuple(again)

    def stats(self) -> dict[str, np.ndarray]:
        """Return the sampler's record of each kept draw, in the order :meth:`kept` holds them (``draws`` values each): :data:`_STATS`."""
        self.kept()
        return self.__dict__["_stats"]

    def __getstate__(self) -> dict:
        """Return the fields for a pickle or a copy, without the caches (the draws are made again, the same, from the seed)."""
        return {
            name: value
            for name, value in self.__dict__.items()
            if name not in ("_sampler_", "_tuning_", "_draws", "_stats")
        }

    def __setstate__(self, state: dict) -> None:
        """Restore from a pickle or a copy, the arrays read-only again."""
        self.__dict__.update(state)
        for value in state.values():
            if isinstance(value, np.ndarray):
                _read_only(value)

    # -- the Solution ------------------------------------------------------------

    def estimate(self) -> np.ndarray:
        """Return the mode."""
        return self.mode

    def sample(self, N: int, rng: np.random.Generator) -> np.ndarray:
        """Return `N` new draws of the posterior (``n x N``): split among the chains as evenly as they go (the first ones a draw more), each from where its tuning for the kept draws ended (the kept draws are made first, if they were not), at its tuned step, side by side in the chains' order."""
        return self._run(self._tunings(), N, rng)[0]

    def _run(
        self, tunings: "tuple[_Tuning, ...]", N: int, rng: np.random.Generator
    ) -> tuple[np.ndarray, dict[str, np.ndarray]]:
        """Return `N` draws made from the chains' `tunings`, as :meth:`sample`'s, and the sampler's record of each (:data:`_STATS`, ``N`` values each, in the same order)."""
        sampler, parts, records = self._sampler(), [], []
        for tuning, count in zip(tunings, _split(N, self.options.chains), strict=True):
            if not count:  # fewer draws asked for than there are chains
                continue
            draws, record = sampler.run(tuning.point, count, rng, tuning.step)
            logger.debug(
                "%d draws of the posterior, accepted at %.2f.",
                count,
                record["acceptance_rate"].mean(),
            )
            parts.append(draws)
            records.append(record)
        stats = {
            name: np.concatenate([record[name] for record in records])
            for name in _STATS
        }
        return np.concatenate(parts, axis=1), stats

    def lengths(self) -> tuple[int, ...]:
        """Return the number of kept draws of each chain, in the order :meth:`kept` holds them side by side."""
        return _split(self.options.draws, self.options.chains)

    def variances(self) -> np.ndarray:
        """Return the variances of the kept draws."""
        kept = self.kept()
        centered = kept - kept.mean(axis=1, keepdims=True)
        return np.einsum("ij,ij->i", centered, centered) / (kept.shape[1] - 1)

    def cov(self, W: LinearOperator | None = None) -> np.ndarray:
        """Return the covariance of the kept draws, ``n x n``, or of their aggregates ``W x``, ``k x k``, with a warning if the chains disagree on an aggregate (the elements' check, at the solve, does not see how they vary together)."""
        kept = self.kept()
        if W is None:
            Y = kept
        else:
            Y = np.asarray(W @ kept, dtype=float)
            _warn_if_aggregates_unmixed(Y, self.lengths(), logger, _UNMIXED_AGGREGATES)
        centered = Y - Y.mean(axis=1, keepdims=True)
        S = centered @ centered.T / (Y.shape[1] - 1)
        return 0.5 * (S + S.T)

    def gain(self, W: LinearOperator | None = None) -> np.ndarray:
        """Return the gain of the problem linearized at the last iterate, or :math:`W` times it."""
        return self.analytic.gain(W)

    def dofs(self) -> float:
        """Return DOFS of the Gaussian at the mode."""
        return self.analytic.dofs()

    def dofs_per_element(self) -> np.ndarray:
        """Return the diagonal of the averaging kernel at the mode."""
        return self.analytic.dofs_per_element()

    def information(self) -> float:
        """Return the information content of the Gaussian at the mode."""
        return self.analytic.information()

    def misfits(self) -> tuple[float, float]:
        """Return the data and the state misfits at the mode."""
        return self.data_misfit, self.state_misfit

    def chi2(self) -> float:
        r"""Return the cost at the mode, :math:`\gamma` times the data misfit plus the state misfit."""
        return self.analytic.gamma * self.data_misfit + self.state_misfit

    def chi2_tests(self) -> dict[str, tuple[float, int]]:
        r"""
        Return Rodgers' four chi-squared tests at the mode, with the covariances of the Gaussian at the mode.

        With :math:`G = J S_0 J^T + S_z/\gamma` (:math:`J` the Jacobian of the
        last linearization): the innovation :math:`d = z - F(x_0) - c` of the
        model itself gives :math:`d^T G^{-1} d`; the residual :math:`r = z -
        F(\hat{x}) - c` gives :math:`u^T G u`, with :math:`u = \gamma S_z^{-1}
        r`; the increment :math:`\hat{x} - x_0 = S_0 y` is measured in the
        directions the observations constrain, as the analytic method does
        (``_increment_chi2_at``), and the modeled increment with it. The
        chi-squared values are those of the model at the mode; the degrees of
        freedom are those of the linearized problem, so the p-values are
        approximate.
        """
        problem, analytic = self.problem, self.analytic
        gamma, m = analytic.gamma, len(problem.obs)
        z = problem.obs.values
        d = z - problem.predict(problem.prior).values
        innovation = float(d @ analytic._precision_times(d[:, None])[:, 0])
        r = z - problem.predict(Vector(self.mode, problem.prior.space)).values
        S_z = problem.obs_cov.data
        u = gamma * np.asarray(S_z.solve(r), dtype=float)
        Jtu = np.asarray(analytic.problem._operator.data._transpose() @ u)
        residual = (
            float(Jtu @ np.asarray(problem.prior_cov.data @ Jtu))
            + float(u @ np.asarray(S_z @ u)) / gamma
        )
        increment, rank = analytic._increment_chi2_at(self.mode_y)
        return {
            "innovation": (innovation, m),
            "residual": (residual, m),
            "modeled_increment": (increment, rank),
            "increment": (increment, rank),
        }

    def iterations(self) -> tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray]:
        r"""Return each step's :math:`d^2` and the cost after it."""
        return self.d2, self.cost

    def linearized(self) -> Problem:
        """Return the problem linearized at the last iterate, which the gain is of."""
        return self.analytic.problem

    def with_data(self, problem: Problem, seed: int | None = None) -> "GaussNewton":
        """
        Return the solution of `problem`, this one's with other observation values: Gauss-Newton again, from the last linearization.

        The first step reuses the factors of the last linearized problem (they
        do not depend on the observations' values), so it costs a product.
        `seed` replaces the options' seed for the sampler (each trial of
        :meth:`Posterior.trials <fips.Posterior.trials>` has its own).
        """
        options = self.options
        if seed is not None:
            options = replace(options, seed=seed)
        return _iterate(problem, options, start=self)


_QUARTER = 0.5 * math.pi
"""The time each draw travels: a quarter period of the Gaussian at the mode, after which a draw of that Gaussian would be independent of its start."""

_TARGET = 0.9
"""The acceptance rate the step is tuned to during the burn-in."""

_JITTER = 0.2
"""Each draw's step is the tuned one times a uniform number within this fraction of 1, so that no path is periodic."""

_MOST_STEPS = 1000
"""The most steps a draw takes: the step is never tuned below a quarter period over this."""

_MANY_STEPS = 200
"""The steps a draw takes at which a solve warns that the posterior bends very much or the Jacobian is wrong."""

_BLOCK = 512
"""The number of velocities the sampler draws in one block."""

_FAILED = (ValueError, FloatingPointError, OverflowError, LinAlgError)
"""What a model (or a transform) raises at a state it cannot model: a path that reaches one is rejected."""

_WIDER = 0.03
"""The fraction of gamma the sampler's Gaussian is solved at where the posterior is not the Gaussian at the mode: the Gaussian at the mode of the problem linearized there, with a thirtieth of the data's weight (chosen on plan 010's measurements, 2026-11-15)."""

_FLAT = 0.1
"""The spread of the remainder (log density) over :data:`_PROBES` draws of the Gaussian at the mode below which the posterior is taken as that Gaussian, and the sampler is split there."""

_PROBES = 16
"""The draws of the Gaussian at the mode the remainder is read at, to choose the Gaussian the sampler is split at."""

_RANK = 10
"""The most directions the sampler's Gaussian is widened along when it is refitted."""

_WIDENS = 2.0
"""The ratio of the draws' variance along a direction to the sampler's Gaussian's above which a refit widens it there."""

_MOST_SEEN = 1000
"""The most draws, of all the chains, the sampler's Gaussian is refitted from: of more, every k-th of each chain's (the refit's cost grows as their square)."""


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class _Point:
    r"""A state of the chain with what the next path needs there: the remainder :math:`R(x)`, its gradient :math:`\rho(x)` and :math:`S' \rho(x)`."""

    x: np.ndarray
    R: float
    rho: np.ndarray
    S_rho: np.ndarray


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class _Tuning:
    """What the burn-in leaves: the tuned step, the state it ended at, and its acceptance rate."""

    step: float
    point: _Point
    acceptance: float


class _Sampler:
    r"""
    Hamiltonian Monte Carlo for the posterior of a problem with a forward model, split at a Gaussian about the mode.

    The posterior is :math:`p(x \mid z) \propto e^{-U(x)}`, :math:`U` half
    the cost. Write it as a Gaussian about the mode times the rest,
    :math:`U = U_G + R`, with :math:`U_G(x) = \tfrac{1}{2} (x - \hat{x})^T
    S'^{-1} (x - \hat{x})`, where :math:`S'^{-1} = S_0^{-1} + \gamma' J_*^T
    S_z^{-1} J_*` is the covariance of the problem linearized at the last
    iterate (:math:`J_*` its Jacobian) solved at :math:`\gamma'`
    (``split``). The momentum's mass is :math:`S'^{-1}`, so a velocity is a
    draw of :math:`N(0, S')`: the analytic method's exact draws less their
    mean (``split.sample``). The dynamics of :math:`U_G` with that mass are a
    rotation, solved exactly, as for the bounded method's sampler:

    .. math::

        x(t) = \hat{x} + (x - \hat{x}) \cos t + v \sin t, \qquad
        v(t) = v \cos t - (x - \hat{x}) \sin t,

    and :math:`R` acts as kicks, :math:`v \leftarrow v - h S' \nabla R`,
    half a step before and after each rotation by :math:`h` (Strang
    splitting: Shahbaba et al., 2014). The path is symplectic and reversible,
    so a Metropolis test on the change of :math:`H = U + \tfrac{1}{2} v^T
    S'^{-1} v` makes the chain exact whatever :math:`S'` and :math:`h` are;
    they set only how fast it mixes.

    **Which Gaussian.** At :math:`\gamma' = \gamma` it is the Gaussian at the
    mode, :math:`\hat{S}` (``analytic``). Where that is the posterior (a
    linear model), :math:`\nabla R = 0`, every path is accepted, and one step
    of a quarter period is an exact, independent draw. Where the posterior
    bends along a curve, it is the wrong one: to reach a far end of the curve
    a path must move far in the directions the data pin down at the mode,
    where :math:`\hat{S}` is narrowest, and its velocities, drawn from
    :math:`\hat{S}`, are smallest there; the chain creeps along the curve and
    can miss an end altogether (issue #203). So the sampler first reads
    :math:`R` at :data:`_PROBES` draws of the Gaussian at the mode: where it
    varies by less than :data:`_FLAT` (the posterior is that Gaussian within a
    tenth in log density), :math:`\gamma' = \gamma`; otherwise :math:`\gamma'
    = ` :data:`_WIDER` :math:`\gamma`, a Gaussian as wide as the prior where
    the data are weak and wider than :math:`\hat{S}` where they pin the state
    down, whose velocities carry the paths along the curve while the kicks of
    the data's term keep them on it. Measured on plan 010's problems (two
    curved pairs against their grids, #223's two strong cases against emcee,
    23 settings of 100 to 900 elements against long solves): the share of
    elements whose spread is more than 10% off fell from a median of 1.6% to
    0.1%, at 1.6 times the model runs in the median (at most 2.8).

    **The refit.** The wider Gaussian is still a guess at the posterior's
    spread, and where the posterior has arms it does not reach, chains
    started apart can settle in different ones. So where the chains' kept
    draws disagree (a split R-hat above :data:`~fips._draws._UNMIXED` on
    any element; :meth:`GaussNewton._disagree`), they refit it
    (:meth:`refit`): :math:`S'' = S' + U U^T`, widened along the
    directions where the draws of all the chains spread more than
    :data:`_WIDENS` times :math:`S'` does, to their spread there, at most
    :data:`_RANK` of them. Written as :math:`U = S' B`, Woodbury gives
    :math:`S''^{-1} = S'^{-1} - B \Lambda^{-1} B^T`, so the remainder below
    gains :math:`\tfrac{1}{2} q^T \Lambda^{-1} q` with :math:`q = B^T (x -
    \hat{x})`, a velocity gains :math:`U \eta`, and a kick needs one
    product with :math:`S'` as before. Each chain then tunes its step again
    over half a burn-in from its last draw, and the kept draws are made
    again with the refitted Gaussian fixed: the first ones are left out as
    the burn-in's are, so the chain is exact as before. Measured on the
    reference suite (``tests/reference``, 2026-11-17, against grids and
    nutpie's long runs): no solve of the problems of 100 to 900 elements
    was drawn again (their draws are as before); on a softplus problem of
    400 elements with a log-std of 1.5, whose chains settle in different
    arms, the standard deviations' error fell from 83% to 2.8% (rms), at
    3.5 to 4.6 times the model runs; the curved pairs of #203 were drawn
    again in 3 and 7 solves of 16, at about 2.2 times the runs, their
    errors 5.6% to 5.2% and 7.8% to 5.2%. Refitting in every burn-in
    instead helped about as much at about twice the runs on every solve
    that bends, right ones included.

    :math:`S_0^{-1}` is never needed. With :math:`\hat{x} = x_0 + S_0
    \hat{y}` (``mode_y``) and every state in :math:`x_0` plus the range of
    :math:`S_0` (the velocities and kicks are), in the state whitened by
    :math:`S_0` the remainder is

    .. math::

        R(x) = \hat{y}^T (x - \hat{x}) + \tfrac{1}{2} \big(\gamma r^T S_z^{-1} r
            - \gamma' d^T S_z^{-1} d\big), \qquad r = z - F(x) - c, \quad
            d = J_* (x - \hat{x}),

    up to a constant; its gradient is :math:`\rho = \hat{y} - \gamma J(x)^T
    S_z^{-1} r - \gamma' J_*^T S_z^{-1} d`, so a kick is :math:`S' \rho`
    (``split._cov_times``). The kinetic energy is never formed either: a kick
    of :math:`h` changes it by :math:`-h \rho^T v + \tfrac{1}{2} h^2 \rho^T S'
    \rho`, and a rotation not at all. A singular :math:`S_0` (elements with
    no prior spread) leaves those elements at the mode.

    Each draw starts from the last with a new velocity and travels a quarter
    period, :data:`_QUARTER`, in steps of the tuned :math:`h` (jittered by
    :data:`_JITTER`). Each chain starts from a draw of the Gaussian at the
    mode (:meth:`start`; one chain alone, from the mode), and its burn-in tunes its own :math:`h` to an
    acceptance rate of :data:`_TARGET` by dual averaging (Hoffman and
    Gelman, 2014), starting from one step a draw; its draws are left out.
    A path the model cannot follow (it overflows) is rejected. A path whose
    energy rose by more than :data:`~fips._draws._DIVERGENT`, or that the
    model could not follow, diverged: the kept draws from one are counted and
    logged at INFO (:func:`~fips._draws._log_divergent`). Each path's record
    (:data:`_STATS`) is kept with its draw (``Posterior.sample_stats``).
    """

    def __init__(self, solution: "GaussNewton"):
        problem, analytic = solution.problem, solution.analytic
        self.problem = problem
        self.analytic = analytic
        self.gamma = analytic.gamma
        self.mode = solution.mode
        self.mode_y = solution.mode_y
        self.mean = analytic.estimate()
        J = analytic.problem._operator.data
        self.J, self.J_T = J, J._transpose()
        self.solve_S_z = problem.obs_cov.data._solver()
        self.z = problem.obs.values
        self.c = problem._offset_values()
        self.split_gamma, self.split = self.gamma, analytic
        empty = np.empty((len(self.mode), 0))
        self.U, self.B, self.widths = empty, empty, np.empty(0)
        options = solution.options
        # The probes' stream is the seed's child after the chains', so the chains' own are unchanged
        probe_rng = np.random.default_rng(
            np.random.SeedSequence(options.seed).spawn(options.chains + 1)[-1]
        )
        spread = self._spread(probe_rng)
        self.bends = spread >= _FLAT
        if self.bends:
            self.split_gamma = _WIDER * self.gamma
            self.split = type(analytic).factor(analytic.problem, self.split_gamma)
        self.split_mean = self.split.estimate()
        logger.info(
            "The posterior sampler is split at the Gaussian at the mode solved at "
            "%.3g times gamma: the remainder varies by %.3g over %d of its draws.",
            self.split_gamma / self.gamma,
            spread,
            _PROBES,
        )

    def _spread(self, rng: np.random.Generator) -> float:
        """Return the standard deviation of the remainder over :data:`_PROBES` draws of the Gaussian at the mode (infinite if the model cannot model one), read while the sampler is split there."""
        draws = self.analytic.sample(_PROBES, rng)
        values = []
        for j in range(_PROBES):
            try:
                values.append(self.point(draws[:, j]).R)
            except _FAILED:
                return math.inf
        return float(np.std(values))

    def point(self, x: np.ndarray) -> _Point:
        r"""
        Return the state `x` with :math:`R`, :math:`\rho` and :math:`S' \rho` there.

        Raises
        ------
        ValueError, FloatingPointError, OverflowError, numpy.linalg.LinAlgError
            If the model cannot model `x`, or the result is not finite.
        """
        problem, gamma, wide = self.problem, self.gamma, self.split_gamma
        with np.errstate(over="raise", invalid="raise"):
            r = self.z - problem._apply(x) - self.c
            dx = x - self.mode
            d = np.asarray(self.J @ dx, dtype=float)
            weighted = np.asarray(self.solve_S_z(np.column_stack([r, d])))
            R = float(self.mode_y @ dx) + 0.5 * (
                gamma * float(r @ weighted[:, 0]) - wide * float(d @ weighted[:, 1])
            )
            rho = (
                self.mode_y
                - gamma * problem._gradient(x, weighted[:, 0])
                - wide * np.asarray(self.J_T @ weighted[:, 1], dtype=float)
            )
            S_rho = self.split._cov_times(rho[:, None])[:, 0]
            if self.widths.size:  # the low-rank part of the refitted Gaussian
                q = (self.B.T @ dx) / self.widths
                R += 0.5 * float(q @ (q * self.widths))
                rho = rho + self.B @ q
                S_rho = S_rho + self.U @ (q + self.U.T @ rho)
        if not (math.isfinite(R) and np.isfinite(S_rho).all()):
            raise FloatingPointError("The remainder is not finite.")
        return _Point(x, R, rho, S_rho)

    def travel(
        self, start: _Point, v: np.ndarray, h: float, steps: int
    ) -> tuple[_Point, float]:
        """Return where the path from `start` with the velocity `v` is after `steps` steps of `h`, and the change of the energy along it."""
        mode = self.mode
        cos, sin = math.cos(h), math.sin(h)
        here = start
        change = -0.5 * h * float(here.rho @ v) + 0.125 * h * h * float(
            here.rho @ here.S_rho
        )
        v = v - 0.5 * h * here.S_rho
        for step in range(steps):
            a = here.x - mode
            x = mode + a * cos + v * sin
            v = v * cos - a * sin
            here = self.point(x)
            kick = h if step < steps - 1 else 0.5 * h
            change += -kick * float(here.rho @ v) + 0.5 * kick * kick * float(
                here.rho @ here.S_rho
            )
            v = v - kick * here.S_rho
        return here, change + (here.R - start.R)

    def move(
        self, here: _Point, v: np.ndarray, test: float, h: float, steps: int
    ) -> tuple[_Point, float, float]:
        """Return the chain's next state from `here` (the path's end if the Metropolis test `test` passes, else `here`), the path's acceptance probability, and the change of the energy along it (infinite if the model could not follow it): above :data:`~fips._draws._DIVERGENT`, or NaN, the path diverged (:func:`_diverged`)."""
        try:
            with np.errstate(over="raise", invalid="raise"):
                end, change = self.travel(here, v, h, steps)
        except _FAILED:
            return here, 0.0, math.inf
        if change <= 0.0:
            alpha = 1.0
        else:  # a NaN change is rejected
            alpha = math.exp(-change) if change < math.inf else 0.0
        return (end if test < alpha else here), alpha, change

    def moves(
        self, count: int, rng: np.random.Generator
    ) -> Iterator[tuple[np.ndarray, float, float]]:
        """Yield `count` velocities, each with the uniform number of its test and its jitter, drawn from `rng` in blocks of :data:`_BLOCK`."""
        done = 0
        while done < count:
            block = min(_BLOCK, count - done)
            V = self.split.sample(block, rng) - self.split_mean[:, None]
            if self.widths.size:
                V += self.U @ rng.standard_normal((self.widths.size, block))
            tests = rng.random(block)
            jitters = rng.uniform(1.0 - _JITTER, 1.0 + _JITTER, block)
            for j in range(block):
                yield V[:, j], float(tests[j]), float(jitters[j])
            done += block

    def start(self, rng: np.random.Generator) -> _Point:
        """
        Return a chain's start: a draw of the Gaussian at the mode, from `rng`.

        A start the model cannot model is moved halfway to the mode until it
        can (the mode itself last). Starts further out, which a check of
        chains usually asks for, are worse here: where the posterior has
        turned away from the Gaussian at the mode, this sampler takes
        hundreds of steps a draw and barely moves, and a chain started there
        stays (seen on a curved two-element posterior, 2026-11-11).
        """
        offset = self.analytic.sample(1, rng)[:, 0] - self.mean
        for _ in range(_HALVINGS):
            try:
                return self.point(self.mode + offset)
            except _FAILED:
                offset = offset / 2
        return self.point(np.array(self.mode))

    def refit(self, seen: list[list[_Point]]) -> None:
        r"""
        Widen the sampler's Gaussian where the draws `seen` (each chain's states) spread more than it does: :math:`S'' = S' + U U^T`.

        The widened Gaussian is written :math:`U = S' B`, so that by Woodbury
        :math:`S''^{-1} = S'^{-1} - B \Lambda^{-1} B^T`, and the sampler needs
        only :math:`B^T (x - \hat{x})` and products with :math:`S'` (no
        :math:`S'^{-1}`, :meth:`point`). The columns of :math:`B` are
        :math:`b_j \sqrt{\lambda_j - 1}`, with :math:`\lambda_j` the ratio of
        the draws' second moment along :math:`b_j` (about the mode) to the
        Gaussian's, :math:`b_j^T S' b_j = 1`, and :math:`\Lambda` their
        diagonal: along :math:`b_j` the widened Gaussian has the draws'
        spread, and elsewhere :math:`S'`'s. The :math:`b_j` are the leading
        ratios (Rayleigh-Ritz) in the span of the leading directions of the
        remainder's gradients at the draws (where the posterior is not the
        Gaussian; measured by :math:`S'`) and of the draws themselves, at
        most :data:`_RANK` of them, each above :data:`_WIDENS`.
        """
        points = [point for chain in seen for point in chain]
        X = np.column_stack([point.x for point in points]) - self.mode[:, None]
        P = np.column_stack([point.rho for point in points])
        SP = np.column_stack([point.S_rho for point in points])
        P -= P.mean(axis=1, keepdims=True)
        SP -= SP.mean(axis=1, keepdims=True)
        directions = 2 * _RANK
        gram = P.T @ SP
        values, vectors = np.linalg.eigh(0.5 * (gram + gram.T))
        top = vectors[:, ::-1][:, :directions]
        draws = np.linalg.svd(X, full_matrices=False)[0][:, :directions]
        dual = np.column_stack([P @ top, draws])
        image = np.column_stack([SP @ top, self.split._cov_times(draws)])
        norms = dual.T @ image
        values, vectors = np.linalg.eigh(0.5 * (norms + norms.T))
        kept = values > len(values) * np.finfo(float).eps * values.max(initial=0.0)
        if not kept.any():  # the draws never moved
            return
        basis = vectors[:, kept] / np.sqrt(
            values[kept]
        )  # dual @ basis is S'-orthonormal
        seen_spread = (dual @ basis).T @ X
        ratios, rotations = np.linalg.eigh(seen_spread @ seen_spread.T / X.shape[1])
        order = np.argsort(ratios)[::-1][:_RANK]
        order = order[ratios[order] > _WIDENS]
        widths = ratios[order]
        a = basis @ rotations[:, order] * np.sqrt(widths - 1.0)
        self.B, self.U, self.widths = dual @ a, image @ a, widths
        if widths.size:
            logger.info(
                "The posterior sampler's Gaussian is widened along %d directions, "
                "from %d draws: by %s in variance.",
                len(widths),
                len(points),
                _shown(list(widths)),
            )

    def tune(
        self,
        burn_in: int,
        rng: np.random.Generator,
        start: _Point | None = None,
        step: float | None = None,
    ) -> _Tuning:
        """
        Return the step tuned over `burn_in` draws from `start` (the mode if None), by dual averaging (Hoffman and Gelman, 2014, algorithm 5) of its logarithm, and where they ended.

        It starts from one step a draw, and the step stays between
        :data:`_QUARTER` over :data:`_MOST_STEPS` and :data:`_QUARTER`. With
        no burn-in, the step is a whole quarter period. With `step`, it starts
        from that step instead (the tuning after a :meth:`refit`).
        """
        here = self.point(np.array(self.mode)) if start is None else start
        longest, shortest = math.log(_QUARTER), math.log(_QUARTER / _MOST_STEPS)
        first = longest if step is None else min(math.log(step), longest)
        target = first + math.log(10.0)
        log_h, log_mean, error, accepted = first, first, 0.0, 0.0
        for m, (v, test, jitter) in enumerate(self.moves(burn_in, rng), start=1):
            steps = _steps(math.exp(log_h))
            here, alpha, _ = self.move(here, v, test, _QUARTER / steps * jitter, steps)
            accepted += alpha
            error += ((_TARGET - alpha) - error) / (m + 10)
            log_h = min(max(target - math.sqrt(m) / 0.05 * error, shortest), longest)
            weight = m**-0.75
            log_mean = weight * log_h + (1.0 - weight) * log_mean
        acceptance = accepted / burn_in if burn_in else math.nan
        return _Tuning(math.exp(log_mean), here, acceptance)

    def run(
        self,
        start: _Point,
        N: int,
        rng: np.random.Generator,
        step: float,
    ) -> tuple[np.ndarray, dict[str, np.ndarray]]:
        """Return `N` draws (``n x N``) of the chain from `start` at the step `step`, with velocities and tests drawn from `rng`, and the record of each draw's path (:data:`_STATS`, ``N`` values each)."""
        steps = _steps(step)
        draws = np.empty((len(start.x), N))
        alphas, changes, sizes = np.empty(N), np.empty(N), np.empty(N)
        here = start
        for j, (v, test, jitter) in enumerate(self.moves(N, rng)):
            sizes[j] = _QUARTER / steps * jitter
            here, alphas[j], changes[j] = self.move(here, v, test, sizes[j], steps)
            draws[:, j] = here.x
        record = {
            "diverging": _diverged(changes),
            "acceptance_rate": alphas,
            "energy_error": changes,
            "n_steps": np.full(N, steps),
            "step_size": sizes,
        }
        return draws, record

def _diverged(change: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    """Return whether each path diverged: its energy rose by more than :data:`~fips._draws._DIVERGENT` (Stan's rule), or by NaN, or without bound (the model could not follow it)."""
    with np.errstate(invalid="ignore"):
        return ~(np.asarray(change) <= _DIVERGENT)


def _split(N: int, chains: int) -> tuple[int, ...]:
    """Return the number of draws each of `chains` chains makes of `N`, as even as they go, the first ones a draw more."""
    each, more = divmod(N, chains)
    return tuple(each + (k < more) for k in range(chains))


def _steps(step: float) -> int:
    """Return the number of steps of about `step` that make a quarter period, at most :data:`_MOST_STEPS`."""
    return max(1, min(_MOST_STEPS, math.ceil(_QUARTER / step - 1e-9)))


def _with_obs(linear: Problem, obs: Vector) -> Problem:
    """Return the linear problem `linear` with the observations `obs`, on its labels."""
    return type(linear)(
        prior=linear.prior,
        prior_cov=linear.prior_cov,
        obs=obs,
        obs_cov=linear.obs_cov,
        forward=linear.forward,
        offset=linear.offset,
    )


def _d2(
    dy: np.ndarray,
    J_step: np.ndarray,
    S_0: LinearOperator,
    solve_S_z: Callable[[np.ndarray], np.ndarray],
    gamma: float,
) -> float:
    r"""
    Return a step's :math:`d^2 = \delta^T (S_0^{-1} + \gamma J^T S_z^{-1} J) \delta`, with neither inverse of :math:`S_0` nor :math:`\hat{S}^{-1}`.

    The step is :math:`\delta = S_0 \Delta y` (both estimates are :math:`x_0 +
    S_0 y`), so its prior term is :math:`\Delta y^T S_0 \Delta y`, and its
    data term :math:`\gamma (J \delta)^T S_z^{-1} (J \delta)`, with `J_step`
    :math:`= J \delta` and `solve_S_z` the solve with :math:`S_z`.
    """
    prior = float(dy @ np.asarray(S_0 @ dy))
    return prior + gamma * float(J_step @ np.asarray(solve_S_z(J_step)))


_HALVINGS = 30
"""The most times a step is halved before the cost stops rising along it."""

_SLOW = 10.0
"""How many times ``tol * n`` the distance left to the mode (in d2), read from the last two steps, may be before a solve warns that it converged slowly."""

_RISE = 1e-12
"""How much the cost may rise along a step, relative, and the step still be taken (roundoff near the mode)."""


def _warn_if_slow(d2s: list[float], limit: float) -> None:
    r"""
    Warn when the steps fell so slowly that the estimate may be further from the mode than the stopping rule meant.

    Rodgers' rule measures the step, not the distance to the mode. When each
    step is a factor :math:`q` of the last (Gauss-Newton converges so, by a
    constant factor, when the model bends much over the posterior's spread),
    the steps still to come add up to :math:`q / (1 - q)` of the last one, so
    the distance left is about :math:`(q / (1 - q))^2 d_k^2` in :math:`d^2`.
    :math:`q` is read from the last two steps, :math:`q^2 = d_k^2 /
    d_{k-1}^2`. It warns when that distance is above ``_SLOW`` times
    ``tol * n`` (`limit`): for a last step near the limit, :math:`q` above
    about 3/4.
    """
    if len(d2s) < 2 or not d2s[-2] > 0:
        return
    q = math.sqrt(d2s[-1] / d2s[-2])
    left = (q / (1 - q)) ** 2 * d2s[-1] if q < 1 else math.inf
    if left <= _SLOW * limit:
        return
    warn(
        logger,
        "Gauss-Newton converged slowly: each step was about %.2f of the last, so "
        "the estimate may still be about %.3g from the mode (as d2), above tol * "
        "n = %.3g. Lower tol to get closer. A model that bends this much over the "
        "posterior's spread is also where the Gaussian at the mode is least right "
        "(check it with post.trials).",
        q,
        left,
        limit,
    )


_NEWTON_ITERATIONS = 50
"""The most conjugate-gradient iterations a Newton step's solve takes; one that has not converged by then gives the Gauss-Newton step."""

_SOLVED = 1e-12
r"""The Newton step is solved when its residual, measured as a :math:`d^2`, is below this fraction of the Gauss-Newton step's :math:`d^2` (a relative error of :math:`10^{-6}`)."""


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class _Taken:
    """A step taken: the fraction of its length, the state it leads to (``x_0 + S_0 y``), the model there, and the cost's terms there (with the weighted residual :math:`S_z^{-1} r`)."""

    fraction: float
    x: np.ndarray
    y: np.ndarray
    modeled: np.ndarray
    data: float
    state: float
    weighted: np.ndarray

    def cost(self, gamma: float) -> float:
        """Return the cost, gamma times the data misfit plus the state misfit."""
        return gamma * self.data + self.state


def _newton(
    analytic: "Whitened | Information",
    curvature: np.ndarray,
    gradient: np.ndarray,
    step: np.ndarray,
) -> tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray] | None:
    r"""
    Return the Newton step :math:`\delta` and :math:`\sigma` with :math:`\delta = \hat{S} \sigma`, or None where the Newton matrix is not positive definite.

    The Newton matrix is the Gauss-Newton one less the model's curvature,
    :math:`A = \hat{S}^{-1} - D` (`curvature` is :math:`D`'s diagonal,
    :math:`\gamma` included), and the step solves :math:`A \delta = b`, with
    `gradient` :math:`b = -g` (minus half the cost's gradient) and `step`
    :math:`\hat{S} b`, the Gauss-Newton step. It is solved by conjugate
    gradients preconditioned by :math:`\hat{S}`, the analytic solve's own
    covariance (``_cov_times``, one product an iteration; in exact arithmetic
    at most one iteration more than the elements :math:`D` is not zero on, and a
    few near the mode, where :math:`D` is small beside :math:`\hat{S}^{-1}`).
    Each search direction :math:`p` is :math:`\hat{S} s` with :math:`s`
    carried beside it, so :math:`A p = s - D p` needs no inverse of
    :math:`\hat{S}` or :math:`S_0`, and :math:`\delta = \hat{S} \sigma` with
    :math:`\sigma` the same sum of the :math:`s`. It stops when the residual
    :math:`\rho` has :math:`\rho^T \hat{S} \rho` (the :math:`d^2` of the
    correction still to make) below :data:`_SOLVED` times the Gauss-Newton
    step's.

    A direction along which :math:`p^T A p \le 0` proves :math:`A` is not
    positive definite, the conjugate gradients' own test at no extra cost
    (a Cholesky of :math:`A` would need it formed, ``n x n``, which the
    whitened form never is): it returns None, and the step is Gauss-Newton's.
    So does a solve that has not converged in :data:`_NEWTON_ITERATIONS`.
    A step it returns is a descent direction of the cost (each :math:`p` it
    took had positive curvature).
    """
    delta, sigma = np.zeros_like(step), np.zeros_like(step)
    rho, zeta = gradient, step
    p, s = zeta, rho
    rz = rz_0 = float(rho @ zeta)
    for k in range(1, _NEWTON_ITERATIONS + 1):
        Dp = curvature * p
        metric = float(p @ s)  # p^T S_hat^-1 p
        bend = metric - float(p @ Dp)
        if not bend > 0.0:
            logger.debug(
                "The Newton matrix is not positive definite along a direction "
                "(its curvature %.3g against Gauss-Newton's %.3g): a Gauss-Newton step.",
                bend,
                metric,
            )
            return None
        alpha = rz / bend
        delta = delta + alpha * p
        sigma = sigma + alpha * s
        rho = rho - alpha * (s - Dp)
        zeta = analytic._cov_times(rho[:, None])[:, 0]
        rz_new = float(rho @ zeta)
        if rz_new <= _SOLVED * rz_0:
            logger.debug("The Newton step solved in %d iterations.", k)
            return delta, sigma
        p = zeta + (rz_new / rz) * p
        s = rho + (rz_new / rz) * s
        rz = rz_new
    logger.debug(
        "The Newton step's solve did not converge in %d iterations: a Gauss-Newton step.",
        _NEWTON_ITERATIONS,
    )
    return None


def _newton_step(
    problem: Problem,
    analytic: "Whitened | Information",
    here: _Taken,
    step: np.ndarray,
    gamma: float,
    solve_S_z: Callable[[np.ndarray], np.ndarray],
) -> tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray, float] | None:
    r"""
    Return Newton's step from `here`, with its :math:`\Delta y` and :math:`d^2`, or None for a model with no curvature or where the Newton matrix is not positive definite (``_newton``).

    `step` is the Gauss-Newton step, :math:`\hat{S} b` with :math:`b = \gamma
    J^T S_z^{-1} r - y` (minus half the cost's gradient; :math:`y` stands for
    :math:`S_0^{-1} (x - x_0)`). With :math:`\delta = \hat{S} \sigma`,
    :math:`\Delta y = \sigma - \gamma J^T S_z^{-1} J \delta`, since
    :math:`S_0 (\sigma - \gamma J^T S_z^{-1} J \hat{S} \sigma) = \hat{S}
    \sigma` (from :math:`\hat{S}^{-1} = S_0^{-1} + \gamma J^T S_z^{-1} J`;
    it holds for a singular :math:`S_0` too), so the next state is still
    :math:`x_0 + S_0 y`.
    """
    curvature = problem._curvature(here.x, here.weighted)
    if curvature is None:
        return None
    J = analytic.problem._operator.data
    J_T = J._transpose()
    gradient = gamma * np.asarray(J_T @ here.weighted, dtype=float) - here.y
    solved = _newton(analytic, gamma * curvature, gradient, step)
    if solved is None:
        return None
    delta, sigma = solved
    J_delta = np.asarray(J @ delta, dtype=float)
    dy = sigma - gamma * np.asarray(J_T @ np.asarray(solve_S_z(J_delta)), dtype=float)
    return (
        delta,
        dy,
        _d2(dy, J_delta, analytic.problem.prior_cov.data, solve_S_z, gamma),
    )


def _along(
    problem: Problem,
    cost: Callable[[np.ndarray, np.ndarray], tuple[float, float, np.ndarray]],
    x: np.ndarray,
    y: np.ndarray,
    step: np.ndarray,
    dy: np.ndarray,
    limit: float,
    gamma: float,
) -> tuple[_Taken | None, Exception | None]:
    """
    Return the step from `x` taken as far along as the cost does not rise above `limit` (any length if `limit` is infinite), halved until it does not, or None if it rises however short; with the model's last failure.

    A step too far can overflow the model (inf, or the NaN of 0 * inf, which a
    Vector refuses): it is taken shorter.
    """
    fraction, failure = 1.0, None
    for _ in range(_HALVINGS):
        x_new, y_new = x + fraction * step, y + fraction * dy
        with np.errstate(over="ignore", invalid="ignore"):
            try:
                modeled = problem._apply(x_new)
            except (ValueError, FloatingPointError, OverflowError) as error:
                failure, modeled = error, None
        if modeled is not None and np.isfinite(modeled).all():
            data, state, weighted = cost(modeled, y_new)
            if gamma * data + state <= limit:
                return _Taken(
                    fraction, x_new, y_new, modeled, data, state, weighted
                ), None
        fraction /= 2
    return None, failure


def _iterate(
    problem: Problem,
    options: GaussNewtonOptions,
    start: GaussNewton | None = None,
    origin: tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray] | None = None,
) -> GaussNewton:
    """
    Return the Gauss-Newton solution of `problem`, from the prior, from the last linearization of `start` (a solution of the same problem with other observations), or from `origin`, a state :math:`x = x_0 + S_0 y` given as ``(x, y)``.

    Each step goes to the analytic estimate of the problem linearized at the
    last estimate; for a model with a curvature (a transform), to Newton's
    estimate instead (``_newton_step``) where its matrix is positive
    definite and its cost is the lower. A step whose cost is higher than the
    last estimate's (it overshot) is halved until it is not. Only a whole
    Gauss-Newton step small enough stops the iterations (so a halved step,
    or a Newton one, never does), and with a curvature only when Newton's
    step is small enough too: where the Newton matrix is nearly singular,
    the Gauss-Newton step is much shorter than the distance to the mode.

    Raises
    ------
    fips.ConvergenceError
        If the iterations have not stopped after ``max_iterations``.
    RuntimeError
        If the cost rises along a step however short.
    """
    gamma = options.gamma
    S_0 = problem.prior_cov.data
    solve_S_z = problem.obs_cov.data._solver()  # factored once for every step
    n = len(problem.prior)
    z = problem.obs.values
    c = problem._offset_values()
    limit = options.tol * n

    def cost(modeled: np.ndarray, y: np.ndarray) -> tuple[float, float, np.ndarray]:
        """Return the data and the state misfits of the state ``x_0 + S_0 y`` that models `modeled`, and the weighted residual ``S_z^-1 r``."""
        r = z - modeled - c
        weighted = np.asarray(solve_S_z(r))
        return float(r @ weighted), float(y @ np.asarray(S_0 @ y)), weighted

    analytic: Whitened | Information | None
    if start is None:
        x, y = origin if origin is not None else (problem.prior.values, np.zeros(n))
        analytic = None
    else:
        x, y = start.point, start.point_y
        linear = _with_obs(start.analytic.problem, problem.obs)
        analytic = start.analytic.with_data(linear)
    modeled = problem._apply(x)
    data, state, weighted = cost(modeled, y)
    here = _Taken(1.0, x, y, modeled, data, state, weighted)
    current = here.cost(gamma)

    d2s: list[float] = []
    whole: list[float] = []  # the d2 the stopping rule compares, of each whole step
    costs: list[float] = []
    newton_steps = 0
    for i in range(1, options.max_iterations + 1):
        if analytic is None:
            analytic = _factor(problem._linearized(here.x, here.modeled), gamma)
        J = analytic.problem._operator.data
        x_full = analytic.estimate()
        y_full = np.asarray(J._transpose() @ analytic._innovation_precision_times())
        step, dy = x_full - here.x, y_full - here.y
        d2 = _d2(dy, np.asarray(J @ step, dtype=float), S_0, solve_S_z, gamma)
        # None for a model without a curvature: Gauss-Newton's step alone
        newton_step = _newton_step(problem, analytic, here, step, gamma, solve_S_z)
        converged = d2 < limit and (newton_step is None or newton_step[2] < limit)
        whole.append(d2 if newton_step is None else max(d2, newton_step[2]))
        rise = math.inf if converged else current + _RISE * abs(current)
        # Newton's step first, so that it is kept on a tie
        candidates = [(step, dy, d2, False)]
        if newton_step is not None and not converged:
            candidates.insert(0, (*newton_step, True))
        taken, failure, newton = None, None, False
        for step_i, dy_i, d2_i, newton_i in candidates:
            along, failure = _along(
                problem, cost, here.x, here.y, step_i, dy_i, rise, gamma
            )
            if along is None:
                logger.debug(
                    "The cost rises along %s step %d however short.",
                    "Newton's" if newton_i else "Gauss-Newton's",
                    i,
                )
            elif taken is None or along.cost(gamma) < taken.cost(gamma):
                taken, d2, newton = along, d2_i, newton_i
        if taken is None:
            raise RuntimeError(
                f"The cost rises along Gauss-Newton's step {i} however short the "
                "step: the Jacobian is not the derivative of the model at the last "
                "estimate (check it with fips.taylortest(problem.forward, x)), or "
                "the model does not give finite observations near it."
            ) from failure
        kind = "Newton" if newton else "Gauss-Newton"
        newton_steps += newton
        if taken.fraction < 1.0:
            logger.debug(
                "%s step %d overshot: taken at %g of its length.",
                kind,
                i,
                taken.fraction,
            )
        trial = taken.cost(gamma)
        d2s.append(d2 * taken.fraction**2)
        costs.append(trial)
        logger.debug(
            "%s step %d: d2 = %.3g (it stops below %.3g), cost %.6g.",
            kind,
            i,
            d2s[-1],
            limit,
            trial,
        )
        if converged:
            logger.info(
                "Gauss-Newton converged in %d steps%s: the last step's d2 is %.3g, "
                "below tol * n = %.3g.",
                i,
                f" ({newton_steps} of them Newton's)" if newton_steps else "",
                d2,
                limit,
            )
            _warn_if_slow(d2s, limit)
            return GaussNewton(
                problem=problem,
                analytic=analytic,
                mode=_read_only(taken.x),
                mode_y=_read_only(taken.y),
                point=_read_only(np.array(here.x)),
                point_y=_read_only(np.array(here.y)),
                d2=_read_only(np.array(d2s)),
                cost=_read_only(np.array(costs)),
                data_misfit=taken.data,
                state_misfit=taken.state,
                options=options,
            )
        here, current, analytic = taken, trial, None
    raise ConvergenceError(_unconverged(options.max_iterations, whole, d2s, limit))


def _shown(values: list[float]) -> str:
    """Return the values as text, the first three and the last four when there are more than eight."""
    shown = [f"{d:.3g}" for d in values]
    if len(shown) > 8:
        shown = [*shown[:3], "...", *shown[-4:]]
    return ", ".join(shown)


def _unconverged(
    steps: int, whole: list[float], taken: list[float], limit: float
) -> str:
    """Return the message of iterations that did not stop: the d2 the rule compared (each whole step's, before its line search), and the d2 of the steps as taken."""
    return (
        f"Gauss-Newton did not converge in {steps} steps: the last whole step's d2 "
        f"is {whole[-1]:.3g}, above tol * n = {limit:.3g} (the rule compares each "
        f"step's d2 before it is shortened: {_shown(whole)}; as taken: "
        f"{_shown(taken)}). If they are still falling, raise max_iterations. A "
        "whole step that does not shrink while the steps taken do (each shortened "
        "to keep the cost falling) points to the Jacobian: check it with "
        "fips.taylortest(problem.forward, x). Or the problem may be too nonlinear "
        "for Gauss-Newton from the prior."
    )


_DISTINCT = 1.0
r"""
The distance between two starts' modes, in posterior standard deviations (:math:`\sqrt{d^2}` in the metric of the Gaussian at the lower one), above which they are different modes.

Two solves that converge to one mode differ by up to about the rule's
:math:`\sqrt{\mathrm{tol} \cdot n}` each, so with a loose ``tol`` (or many
elements) two end points within three times that can be one mode or two:
``_starts`` solves both again, to a ``tol`` at which that is below this
cutoff, and compares their modes.
"""


def _starts(
    problem: Problem, options: GaussNewtonOptions, solved: GaussNewton
) -> GaussNewton:
    r"""
    Return the solution `solved` (from the prior mean), or the lowest-cost mode another start ended at where that is a different one, with a warning when the starts end at different modes.

    The other starts are draws of the prior, :math:`x = x_0 + R w`, each
    with :math:`y = S_0^{-1} (x - x_0)` (one solve with the prior covariance
    for them all, quietly: a nearly singular one falls back to
    :math:`LDL^T`, whose :math:`y` still gives :math:`x`). An element of
    zero prior variance is at its prior mean in every draw, so its :math:`y`
    is 0 and the solve is with the other elements' prior covariance; a prior
    singular beyond its zero variances draws no start, with a warning. The
    draws come from the seed's child after the
    sampler's (so the chains' streams are unchanged). Two starts end at
    different modes when they end more than :data:`_DISTINCT` posterior
    standard deviations apart (``_d2``, in the metric of the problem
    linearized at the lower one). With a loose ``tol``, two solves of one
    mode can end up to :math:`3 \sqrt{\mathrm{tol} \cdot n}` apart, which
    cannot tell one mode from two: two starts that end between
    :data:`_DISTINCT` and that far apart are each solved again from where
    they ended, to the ``tol`` at which that distance is :data:`_DISTINCT`,
    and the new end points are compared. Several modes are then ranked by
    their costs at the new end points too.
    A start that does not converge, or that the model cannot run from, is
    logged and left out. The other starts run quietly (their own warnings
    are logged at INFO: they are checks); a start whose mode is taken is
    solved again aloud, so that the answer's warnings are given.
    """
    rng = np.random.default_rng(
        np.random.SeedSequence(options.seed).spawn(2 * options.chains + 2)[-1]
    )
    x_0 = problem.prior.values
    S_0 = problem.prior_cov.data
    solve_S_z = problem.obs_cov.data._solver()
    gamma = options.gamma
    apart = max(_DISTINCT, 3.0 * math.sqrt(options.tol * len(x_0)))
    # the tol at which two solves of one mode end within _DISTINCT
    tight = replace(options, tol=min(options.tol, (_DISTINCT / 3.0) ** 2 / len(x_0)))
    # by id: the solution (kept, so that its id is not reused) and it solved again
    polished: dict[int, tuple[GaussNewton, GaussNewton]] = {}

    def cost(solution: GaussNewton) -> float:
        """Return the cost at the solution's mode."""
        return gamma * solution.data_misfit + solution.state_misfit

    def distance(a: GaussNewton, b: GaussNewton) -> float:
        """Return how far apart the modes of `a` and `b` are, in standard deviations of the Gaussian at the lower one."""
        low = a if cost(a) <= cost(b) else b
        step = b.mode - a.mode
        J = low.analytic.problem._operator.data
        J_step = np.asarray(J @ step, dtype=float)
        return math.sqrt(
            max(_d2(b.mode_y - a.mode_y, J_step, S_0, solve_S_z, gamma), 0.0)
        )

    def polish(solution: GaussNewton) -> GaussNewton:
        """Return the solution solved again from its mode to the tight tol (itself if `tol` is tight, or if that fails)."""
        if apart == _DISTINCT:
            return solution
        if id(solution) not in polished:
            again = solution
            try:
                with _quietly():
                    again = _iterate(
                        problem, tight, origin=(solution.mode, solution.mode_y)
                    )
            except (ConvergenceError, RuntimeError, *_FAILED) as err:
                logger.info(
                    "Gauss-Newton to tol=%g did not converge: %s", tight.tol, err
                )
            polished[id(solution)] = (solution, again)
        return polished[id(solution)][1]

    def same(a: GaussNewton, b: GaussNewton) -> bool:
        """Return whether `a` and `b` end at one mode, solving both again to the tight tol where their distance cannot tell."""
        d = distance(a, b)
        if d > apart:
            return False
        return d <= _DISTINCT or distance(polish(a), polish(b)) <= _DISTINCT

    offsets = np.column_stack(
        [problem.prior_cov.sample(rng).values for _ in range(1, options.starts)]
    )
    # an element of zero variance is at its prior mean in every draw: its y is 0
    varies = np.flatnonzero(S_0.diagonal() > 0)
    offsets[np.setdiff1d(np.arange(len(x_0)), varies)] = 0.0
    ys = np.zeros_like(offsets)
    part = S_0 if len(varies) == len(x_0) else S_0._select(varies, varies)
    try:
        with _quietly():  # a prior nearly singular: LDL^T's y is still x's, to roundoff
            ys[varies] = np.asarray(part.solve(offsets[varies]), dtype=float).reshape(
                len(varies), -1
            )
    except LinAlgError:
        warn(
            logger,
            "Gauss-Newton ran from the prior mean alone, not from %d draws of the "
            "prior as well: the prior covariance is singular beyond its zero "
            "variances, so the solve with it for the draws' starts (y in "
            "x = x_0 + S_0 y) failed. Whether the posterior has other modes was "
            "not checked.",
            options.starts - 1,
        )
        return solved
    modes, failed = [solved], 0
    origins: dict[int, tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray]] = {}
    for k in range(1, options.starts):
        x, y = x_0 + offsets[:, k - 1], ys[:, k - 1]
        try:
            with _quietly():
                other = _iterate(problem, options, origin=(x, y))
            origins[id(other)] = (x, y)
        except (ConvergenceError, RuntimeError, *_FAILED) as err:
            failed += 1
            logger.info(
                "Gauss-Newton from the prior draw %d did not end at a mode: %s", k, err
            )
            continue
        if not any(same(other, mode) for mode in modes):
            modes.append(other)
        else:
            logger.debug(
                "Gauss-Newton from the prior draw %d ended at a mode already found.", k
            )
    ran = options.starts - failed
    if len(modes) == 1:
        logger.info(
            "Gauss-Newton from the prior mean and %d draws of the prior ended at one "
            "mode%s.",
            options.starts - 1,
            f" ({failed} of the draws did not converge)" if failed else "",
        )
        return solved
    costs = [cost(polish(mode)) for mode in modes]
    best = modes[int(np.argmin(costs))]
    far = max(
        distance(polish(best), polish(mode)) for mode in modes if mode is not best
    )
    warn(
        logger,
        "Gauss-Newton from %d starts (the prior mean and %d draws of the prior) "
        "ended at %d modes, up to %.3g posterior standard deviations from the one "
        "of lowest cost (costs %s): the posterior has several modes. The estimate "
        "is the one of lowest cost%s; the draws, split at the Gaussian there, do not "
        "reach the others, so its variances and intervals are of that mode alone. "
        "More observations or a narrower prior can rule the others out.",
        ran,
        ran - 1,
        len(modes),
        far,
        ", ".join(f"{c:.6g}" for c in sorted(costs)),
        "" if best is solved else " (not the one from the prior mean)",
    )
    if best is solved:
        return best
    return _iterate(problem, options, origin=origins[id(best)])  # aloud: its warnings


def _gauss_newton(problem: Problem, options: GaussNewtonOptions) -> Posterior:
    """Find the mode by Gauss-Newton, from the prior mean and the `starts` - 1 draws of the prior; with a forward operator, the analytic posterior itself."""
    if isinstance(problem.forward, Operator):
        return Posterior(problem, _factor(problem, options.gamma), options)
    solved = _iterate(problem, options)
    if options.starts > 1:
        solved = _starts(problem, options, solved)
    return Posterior(problem, solved, options)
