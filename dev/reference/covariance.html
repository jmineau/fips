Covariances#

A Covariance is a symmetric Operator from a Space to itself: the prior covariance or the observation covariance of a problem. It keeps its matrix in the cheapest form (a diagonal covariance is its variances, a Kronecker one its factors, independent blocks each their own form) and adds what a covariance needs: its variances, solves, the log-determinant, the quadratic form of its inverse, a square root and a draw from it. A dense or sparse matrix must be symmetric, to 1e-10 of its largest entry; one that is not (an upper triangle, say) raises, naming the two entries. A matrix given to it must also be positive semidefinite, to roundoff: one with a clearly negative eigenvalue raises, naming the smallest. The check builds the square root Covariance.sample() draws with, and keeps it. A covariance a model builds is not checked.

In [1]: import pandas as pd

In [2]: import fips

In [3]: flux = fips.Space({"flux": pd.Index(["a", "b"], name="cell")})

In [4]: bias = fips.Space({"bias": pd.Index(["north"], name="site")})

Covariance#

Constructor#

Covariance(data, space[, name])

A covariance: a symmetric operator from a Space to itself.

Covariance.diagonal(values[, space, name])

Build a diagonal covariance from its variances.

Covariance.block_diag(*covariances[, name])

Put independent covariances side by side: each on its own blocks, zero between.

Attributes#

Covariance.space

The labels of both axes.

Covariance.variances

the diagonal, as a vector on the covariance's space.

Linear algebra#

Covariance.solve(b)

Return \(S^{-1} b\), for a positive-definite covariance \(S\).

Covariance.logdet()

Return log(det(S)), from the form's factors (see fips.linalg.LinearOperator.logdet()).

Covariance.quadratic_form(x)

Return \(x^T S^{-1} x\), the squared Mahalanobis length of x.

Covariance.sqrt()

Return a square root \(L\), with \(L L^T = S\).

Covariance.aggregate(W)

Return the covariance of aggregates, \(W S W^T\).

Random draws#

Covariance.sample([rng])

Return one draw from \(N(0, S)\), a vector on the covariance's space.

Covariance models#

A correlation model says how correlated two labels are, from the coordinates on some levels of their space; model.with_std(std) makes it a covariance. With the standard deviations as a Vector, that is a Covariance at once; with a number, or a pandas Series on the block’s labels (lined up by label; a plain array is refused), a CovarianceModel, which build() puts on a space. Models add, and Covariance.block_diag() takes them by block name.

Kernels#

Exponential(levels, scale, *[, within, metric])

An exponential correlation, \(\exp(-d / \ell)\) at a distance \(d\).

Gaussian(levels, scale, *[, within, metric])

A Gaussian correlation, \(\exp(-d^2 / (2 \ell^2))\) at a distance \(d\).

Matern(levels, scale[, nu, within, metric])

A Matérn correlation, of smoothness \(\nu\).

Constant(*[, within])

A correlation of one between every two labels (of a group, with within).

Combinations#

Independent()

The identity correlation: each label is correlated with itself only.

Kronecker(*factors)

The product of correlation models on separate levels, kept as its factors.

Parameters#

Exponential.levels

The levels of the labels the correlation is a function of.

Exponential.scale

The length scale, a time for a datetime or timedelta level, else in the levels' units (km on the Earth).

Exponential.metric

How the distance is measured, "euclidean", "haversine" or "chordal".

Exponential.within

The levels on which two labels must be the same to be correlated ("time.hour" is an attribute of a datetime level).

Matern.nu

The smoothness, 1/2 for the exponential and smoother as it grows.

Constant.within

The levels on which two labels must be the same to be correlated ("time.hour" is an attribute of a datetime level).

Kronecker.factors

The factors, each a model on its own levels.

Kronecker.levels

The levels the factors read, factor by factor.

The base of every model#

CorrelationModel()

A correlation model: how correlated two labels are, from their coordinates.

CorrelationModel.levels

The levels of the labels the correlation is a function of.

CorrelationModel.correlation(coords)

Return the correlation between the rows of coords.

CorrelationModel.with_std(std)

Return the covariance of this correlation with standard deviations std.

A covariance waiting for its space#

CovarianceModel()

A covariance without its labels: it becomes a Covariance on a space.

CovarianceModel.build(space[, name])

Return the covariance on space.

CovarianceModel.__add__(other)

Return the sum with a covariance model or a covariance.

Distances#

The kernels measure distance with these functions of fips.covariance.distances. Points on the Earth are latitude and longitude in degrees, and their distances are in kilometers on a sphere of radius 6371 km.

haversine_matrix(lats, lons)

Calculate the pairwise great-circle (haversine) distance matrix between points.

chordal_matrix(lats, lons)

Calculate the pairwise chordal distance matrix between points on the Earth.

time_diff_matrix(times)

Calculate the absolute time difference between each pair of times.
